## Introduction

I work at the interface of quantum many-body physics, quantum optics, AMO physics, and nonequilibrium quantum dynamics, asking how interactions, driving, quantum statistics, and dissipation can be harnessed to create, probe, and control quantum phenomena.

## Research statement

My work connects formal structure to observables: how a driven system exchanges energy with its environment, how fractional statistics reconstruct momentum correlations, and how effective models make strongly interacting dynamics tractable.

## Biography

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Center for Quantum Research and Technology and the Homer L. Dodge Department of Physics and Astronomy at the University of Oklahoma, working with Prof. Doerte Blume. I received my Ph.D. in Physics from Rice University in May 2024, with Prof. Han Pu, for work on quantum phases and dynamics of strongly interacting spinor gases in one-dimensional optical lattices.

My research spans driven open systems and quantum control, anyonic quantum matter, strongly correlated ultracold gases and transport, and driven Rydberg systems. I combine analytical open-system techniques with exact diagonalization, tensor networks, variational Monte Carlo, and effective-Hamiltonian methods to connect microscopic models with experimentally accessible observables.

Alongside research, I teach advanced undergraduate physics, co-mentor student researchers, and develop quantum-education activities for high-school STEM teachers.
