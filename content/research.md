## Chapter title text

Driven open systems

## Chapter title heading

How can a master equation retain the physics of strong, time-dependent driving?

## Research 1 narrative and topics

I examine how coherent driving reshapes dissipation, steady states, dynamics, and spectroscopic response in multilevel open quantum systems. Dynamical invariants provide a route to time-dependent master equations that incorporate drive-modified system–environment interactions beyond conventional weak- or strong-driving limits.

My work combines Liouville-space methods, quantum regression, Laplace and Fourier transforms, and perturbative expansions to analyze transient dynamics, emission and absorption spectra, and bath-induced cross-correlations in structured reservoirs and atom–cavity systems.

- Dynamical invariants
- Master-equation validity
- Structured reservoirs
- Quantum control & spectra

## Chapter title text 2

Ultracold quantum matter

## Chapter title heading 2

Which effective degrees of freedom make strong correlations understandable?

## Research 2 narrative and topics

I examine how strong interactions, particle statistics, spin degrees of freedom, and geometry shape quantum phases and transport in multicomponent ultracold matter. I connect microscopic Bose– and Fermi–Hubbard models with tractable effective spin descriptions for equilibrium and nonequilibrium calculations.

Applications include coupled spin and charge transport, Drude weights, thermoelectric response, and Bose gases with physical- and synthetic-dimension hopping. Computational approaches include DMRG, TDVP, exact diagonalization, spin-wave theory, and variational Monte Carlo with entangled plaquette states.

- Spin & charge transport
- Hubbard & spin-chain models
- Synthetic dimensions
- Correlated quantum phases

## Chapter title text 3

Anyonic systems

## Chapter title heading 3

How does fractional exchange statistics reorganize correlations and transport?

## Research 3 narrative and topics

I study how tunable statistical phases shape equilibrium states, dynamics, and transport in multicomponent one-dimensional quantum matter. Combining Hubbard and generalized effective spin-chain descriptions with particle mappings connects few- and many-body systems and separates exchange statistics from bosonic or fermionic on-site constraints.

I examine momentum distributions, expansion dynamics, dipole oscillations, and force-induced Bragg reflection, alongside composite anyons and quantum mixtures. The aim is to identify measurable statistical signatures and explore how statistical-phase sensitivity and phase separation can support quantum control and sensing.

- Multicomponent & composite anyons
- Generalized particle mappings
- Momentum reconstruction
- Statistical-phase control

## Chapter title text 4

Driven Rydberg systems

## Chapter title heading 4

How do periodic driving and long-range interactions control collective dynamics?

## Research 4 narrative and topics

I study how coherent driving, long-range interactions, and dissipation shape state control in Rydberg systems. This work connects periodically driven arrays, Rydberg-dressed gases, and multilevel light–matter coupling schemes to understand blockade, dynamical localization, optical response, and interaction-modified steady states.

Methods include Floquet theory, Bessel-function expansions, transfer matrices, and effective Hamiltonians. My experience also covers electromagnetically induced transparency, three- and four-level coupling schemes, and van der Waals interaction calculations.

- Floquet control
- Rydberg blockade & dressing
- Dynamical localization
- Light–matter interactions
